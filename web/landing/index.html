Philosophy

Friction, not force.

Most blockers are a locked door: on or off, all or nothing, one password away from defeat. Discnct is built on a different bet — that the right amount of resistance, matched to how bad the habit is, works better than a wall you'll just tear down.

01

You choose the level

Start surgical. Escalate only when a lighter touch stops being enough — never further than you need.

02

No dark patterns

No streaks to protect, no guilt copy, no ads. It either helps you scroll less or it doesn't — nothing else it's optimizing for.

03

Reversible, on purpose

Every level can be turned back off. The goal is a habit you don't need the app for anymore, not a cage.

How it works

Three levels, escalating on purpose.

Each level is a complete, independent tool — turn on just the one that matches your problem, or stack all three.

LEVEL 01

Reel Blocker

Two switches per app. Reels & Shorts bounces you straight back out the moment a vertical feed opens — no block screen, nothing to dismiss. Main feed cover puts a solid pane over the timeline and stories where they sit. DMs, search, posting and profiles keep working either way. There's no way to earn your way in: to get them back, you turn the switch off.

Get it

Not on the Play Store — installed directly.

Discnct is sideloaded on purpose: no store review delay, no tracking SDKs a store would require. Android will ask you to allow installs from this source — that is the only setting you need to change.

“Blocked by work policy”

That comes from your employer's or school's device management, not from Discnct. An admin has locked Play Protect, and nothing on the phone can unlock it.

You don't need to. Changing Play Protect was never part of installing Discnct. When it flags an app it doesn't recognise, the warning itself has the way through: tap More details, then Install anyway.

If your phone has a work profile — the apps with a little briefcase on them — install Discnct on the personal side, using your personal browser rather than the work one. If the whole phone is company-managed, Discnct can't run on it: an admin can withhold the Accessibility permission it needs. Use a personal phone for this one.

The Accessibility switch is greyed out

Android labels it a “Restricted setting” for anything installed outside the Play Store. Nothing is broken and Discnct isn't being denied — the switch just has to be unlocked once, by hand.

Settings › Apps › Discnct › ⋮ › Allow restricted settings

Then go back and turn Accessibility on. Discnct shows these same steps the first time you open it, so there's nothing to memorise here.
